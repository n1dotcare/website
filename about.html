Product principles

Four commitments you can test in n1.

They shape how n1 handles source evidence, review state, time, and its own limits.

See the four commitments Open a sample report
How we build

Each commitment has a visible test.

01

The source stays attached

Why
A finding is hard to check without its provenance.
In n1
Findings keep a link to their source page.
Review the provenance example →
02

Drafts stay under clinician review

Why
Sorted information isn't the same as a clinical conclusion.
In n1
The clinician checks sources, edits the draft, and picks what's shared.
Check the review states →
03

Time stays visible

Why
A latest result can hide the direction and timing of change.
In n1
Dates stay with values so you can check change with context.
Open the health-over-time example →
04

The scope stays explicit

Why
A draft note isn't a reviewed clinical conclusion.
In n1
n1 can draft notes and recommendations. A clinician must check, edit, and decide how to use them.
Review the report labels →
Hold us to these commitments

Ask four questions in every workflow.

  1. 01Can I open the source page?
  2. 02Can I see what still needs review?
  3. 03Can I follow the recorded timeline?
  4. 04Is n1’s role separate from clinical judgement?
